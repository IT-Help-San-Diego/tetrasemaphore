## 3. One set of checks for every published text
Carey's five checks for his quotes apply to everything published, whoever wrote it:
1. **Science:** true, or plainly an opinion. Every factual claim is either proved or sourced from a source actually read.
2. **Public:** no profanity, no insults.
3. **Health:** nothing about anyone's health.
4. **Privacy:** no client and no well-known person named or identifiable, until they and he are dead. The check is about private conversations,
   not the public record (Carey, 21 and 22 October 2026): nothing said in private about a client, a well-known person or anyone else Carey knows
   is published, and nothing already public is hidden. Published work is cited with its authors' names, and a public person's public words and
   acts are cited the same way, with their source.
5. **Mission:** about the method, with no carrier-colour lean. In the project's own voice, no commands (the voice rule).

## 4. Every claim says who made it
- **The decision log:** for each decision, the date, the evidence, who proposed it, who checked it, and who decided.
- **Labels:** text drafted by an AI says so. Carey's words are verbatim and dated, with spelling and light grammar edits only.

## 5. Nobody certifies their own work
- **Builds:** Claude Code builds; Science checks from the files.
- **Proofs:** the checker, plus a re-run and a red team by another lane.
- **Notes and rulings:** Science drafts; Claude Code and Hermes red-team; Carey decides.

## 7. Licensing, authorship and ownership (decided by Carey, 21 October 2026; not legal advice)
- **Text and art: CC BY 4.0,** as both repos have now, except the owl drawing (below). A Creative Commons licence cannot be revoked for what
  has been released under it, so nobody can later lock up the released work, the originator included.
- **Not covered: the owl drawing.** The owl in the Owl Semaphore is a licensed stock image (VectorStock 48792427). Its licence allows the
  image in designs, websites and merchandise but not making the image itself available for others to reuse, so no open licence covers it.
  Claude Code first raised the conflict with the Owl Semaphore repository's CC BY 4.0 notice as an open question on 9 September 2026; it was
  still uncorrected on 21 October, when Science found it. Carey decided the fix that day (question 8b): the repository's notice gains an
  exception for the owl, the four files that show the owl on its own leave its current version, and its CHANGELOG discloses the error. Its
  history is not rewritten: earlier copies remain, and the owl in them is not covered either. Carey files the licence receipt with the
  record when he finds it.
- **No owl in anything new.** Carey, 21 October 2026: "we shouldn't publish anything Owl ever again then. Just words to point them to the
  new thing". The Tetrasemaphore repository, its DOI deposit, the website, the phone screens, the shirts and the published working record
  carry no owl image. The old Owl Semaphore page stays as published, with its notice and its corrected sentences: words that point to the
  new work. The Tetrasemaphore's semaphores contain no part of the owl: their ring is computed by the project's own code (provenance record,
  September 2026), and the recipe is in each master file's own description.
- **Code** (the Lean proofs, the checker, the build scripts): **Apache 2.0,** on Science's recommendation, because Creative Commons advises
  against its licences for software. Lean and Mathlib use Apache 2.0, and its patent grant protects anyone who uses or builds on the code.
- **Credit:** CC BY requires credit to the originator; CC0 would have waived even that. Carey chose CC BY.
- **Author:** Carey is the one author on the DOI and the repository; the contributions statement (§2) names each AI system and what it did.
- **Ownership:** Carey owns the work personally and grants the licences himself. IT Help San Diego Inc., where he has the final say and is
  the majority shareholder, paid for the AI services; paying for tools does not make the payer the owner. A one-page record, signed for the
  company, transfers to Carey any rights the company might have in the work; he makes it when he chooses, with an attorney when he can, and
  the release does not wait for it. If an organisation takes on the work's stewardship later, the copyright can be transferred then;
  everything already released stays under its licences.

## 11. How this project publishes (Carey, 21 October 2026) In his words, where they pass the five checks: "All I can do is reveal the process." "Every
single time that I publish, I want to show an improvement to my science." He asks to be vetted more severely than usual, not less: "I can't wait to
have an actual human care about what I'm doing and get a real peer review because I want to make it better." His full statement is kept in the reserved
record (rule 4). Seven rules follow from it:
1. **Versions, never silent edits.** Each public text and file carries a version and a date. Every version stays available and citable: a Zenodo
   DOI per version under one concept DOI, and the repository's history.
2. **Corrections are notes on the record.** Each says what was wrong, what it says now, who found it and when, and links both versions.
   - **The terms.** In this plan, a *correction* fixes errors that leave the main findings standing. A *retraction* withdraws a work whose findings
     and conclusions cannot be relied upon; COPE's stated purpose for it is to alert readers to such articles (COPE Retraction guidelines, version
     3, August 2025, doi:10.24318/cope.2019.1.4). Here the retracted work stays on the record, labelled as retracted (rule 1).
   - **Nothing published so far needs a retraction.** The Owl Semaphore's stories get corrections. The project as a whole has a *successor*, the
     Tetrasemaphore, linked both ways (§1), which is neither a correction nor a retraction.
3. **The process is published with the result.** At release: the decision log (Carey's choices, verbatim and dated, with the AI system that
   proposed or checked each one), the failure-modes register, the test register, the proofs and their red team, and the curated exchanges of §9.
   - **Precedent:** the Journal of Open Source Software runs every review as a public GitHub issue, so the whole review record stays public
     (Smith et al. 2018, PeerJ Computer Science 4:e147, doi:10.7717/peerj-cs.147).
   - **What this project adds:** the reasons behind each decision, including the dead ends, not only the change itself.
4. **Reserved, not hidden.** Carey: "not obfuscated, not omitted, but reserved for the appropriate time." The rule follows the research-data
   principle "as open as possible, as closed as necessary" (Horizon Europe):
   - *What may be reserved:* only material that fails the health, privacy or public check (anyone's health, other people's and clients' privacy,
     profanity or insults), or that a lawyer advises against releasing. Nothing else.
   - *What is published about it:* an index saying that the material exists, its dates and size, which lane recorded it, and why it is reserved.
   - *Who decides when it opens:* Carey; after his death, his written instruction and his executor (§9).
   - *It is never deleted, and no public claim rests on it.* Every claim can be checked from what is published.
5. **Claims follow tests.** Results are stated only for the rungs of the ladder that have passed (test register §7). Everything else is labelled as
   proposed.
6. **Vetting is invited.** A standing invitation to review and red-team the work. Each review is published with the reviewer's permission and
   answered in the decision log.
7. **Respect.** The method asks no one to take on or give up a belief. It makes no claims about anyone's religion and is aimed at no one.
   Carey, 20 October 2026: "the goal can't be to destroy what they believe in, and it can't be to filter it or strip it because that's going
   to be just as horrible. And by the way, why would we want to strip away the history and the how it happened?"

**Where the statement sharpens this plan:**
- The decision log and the curated exchanges are published at the first release, not later. They are the evidence of the human decisions behind
  the work, which answers the "it's only AI" reading Carey expects.
- Every release carries a changelog.
- Review is invited, not only accepted.

**Where it needs care** (kept out of public text by his own checks):
- "Retraction" for routine improvements: the word is "correction", or "new version".
- "Reveal everything": true only with rule 4 beside it.
- Saying the project is better than science, or describing closed or commercial science: these are unsourced claims about others, and they read
  as an attack, which is the carrier colour he wants to avoid.
- "This is how humans organize thoughts in their mind": a hypothesis, tested at ladder rungs 3 and 4.
