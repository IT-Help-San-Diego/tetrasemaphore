# The Tetrasemaphore: public decision log

**What this is.** Every decision recorded for the Tetrasemaphore, in date order, with what was decided, who proposed or found it, and who decided
it. Carey James Balboa decided each one. The AI systems that worked on the project proposed, built, measured, checked and red-teamed; where one of
them was corrected, or corrected someone else, the entry says so.

**It is screened.** Claude Science assembled this log from the decision page's own export, from Science's file of the answers Carey gave in chat,
from the vetted quote bank and from the reading record of the Google AI Mode conversations, and screened every line against Carey's five checks:
science (true, or plainly an opinion), public (no profanity, no insults), health (nothing about anyone's health, his included), privacy (no client
and no well-known person named or identifiable, until he and they are both dead), mission (about the method, with no carrier-colour lean). 
The check is about private conversations, not the public record: nothing said in private about a client, a well-known person or anyone
else Carey knows is published, and nothing already public is hidden. Published work is cited with its authors' names, and a public person's
public words and acts are cited the same way, with their source.

Some decisions are published here in plain words rather than in his own, because his words carry profanity, or concern private matters, his own or other people's. Where his words are quoted they are verbatim, with spelling and light grammar edits only.

**The record behind it.** The full working record exists: the session transcripts of every lane, the lane ledger and the decision files. The
release plan calls it "Kept privately, never deleted". Nothing published here depends on the part that is not published, and every claim in it can
be checked from what is published.

**How to read it.**
- *Decided by* is Carey for every entry. *Proposed by* or *found by* names the contributor whose work put the question in front of him.
- A decision that a later one replaced says so and points to the later entry. A pick he called a mistake is marked **void**.
- Tags mark where the work went both ways: **Carey corrected the AI**, **the AI corrected Carey**, **one AI corrected another**, **Carey corrected
  himself**, **an AI corrected itself**.
- **Dates.** Card and chat entries carry the date in UTC, from the timestamps in the records. Entries drawn from the quote bank carry the date as
  that bank records it, which is a US/Pacific calendar date; a Pacific date can fall on the next UTC day, so those dates are marked *(Pacific)*.
- For decisions before October, the sources read for this log record Carey's words and what was decided, and not always who proposed it. Those
  entries say so.

---

**What is in it.** 146 entries, 5 September to 22 October 2026, from the decision page's 24 answered cards, the numbered chat decisions in
Science's file (items 1 to 148), and the dated records the release plan's failure-modes register cites. 45 of the entries carry a correction
tag, 53 tags in all: 17 where Carey corrected an AI, 7 where he corrected himself, 11 where an AI corrected itself, 6 where an AI corrected
Carey or the story the team was telling, and 12 where one AI corrected another. One pick is marked void. The log closes with what is still
open, where the decisions came from, and the words the project took from another contributor.

### 2026-09-17 — The search for a cultural carrier ends
The Andean edition had drawn on Muisca imagery, a living people of the Colombian Andes, on 16 and 17 September. The search for a carrier in any
culture stopped on 17 September and the notation itself became the carrier. Decided by Carey. The mandate that stands behind the decision — the one ONIC published on 4 May 2024, signed by the authorities of four Muisca resguardos, which
condemns the use of their knowledge systems and cultural identity for commercial gain — was read first-hand by Claude Code on 17 September, and
read again by Claude Science on 11 October in an archived copy, when the public note's owl section was written; see the entry of that date.

The fold test applies to the centre mark, and the meander ring stays as it is. Decided by Carey: "And by the way, the center should be the thing
you fold, not the meander ring or anything else. That stays exactly as it is. So that's already finished."

### 2026-09-29 (Pacific) — Why 0.9998 was not 1
Carey questioned a measured self-similarity of 0.9998 rather than accepting it: "why can't it be absolutely perfect?" Claude Science traced the gap
to the renderer rather than the artwork. Decided by Carey. **Carey corrected the AI** — the number had been reported as a property of the art.

## October 2026, 3 to 8 October

### 2026-10-03 (Pacific) — The project's stated view of mathematics
The project says plainly that it takes mathematics to be discovered rather than invented, as Carey's view and not as a finding. In his words: "It's
got to still be what it should be: something to help the future of humanity, and Earth, and thinking, period. And it should be bigger than even
Earth. It should just be what is. That's what we do when we discover math. Honor finally what is." Decided by Carey. No check in the public note
depends on a reader sharing it.

### 2026-10-04 (Pacific) — The twins rule
The two σ coins differ only by their axis. Carey asked the question: "Must the two coins be twins?" The algebra answered it, and Claude Science
withdrew the answer it had given earlier, that the choice was his eye's call. Decided by Carey. **An AI corrected itself**, prompted by his
question.

### 2026-10-04 (Pacific) — The camera test, as he set it
A reader with a phone should get back the notation, not merely a formula. In his words: "if they scan it with a camera system, would it find it as
a valid mathematical formula? And, well, Google so far said yes, and so that's where we are still in our thinking. So what is the math answer?"
Decided by Carey. The later rule, added by Claude Science and kept with it: finding "a formula" is not a pass; the reader has to return σₕ, σᵥ
or C₂. Superseded on the result by the entry of 22 October, *The camera test, run and reported*.

### 2026-10-04 (Pacific) — The voice rule: teach why, do not command
The coins and their texts carry no commands. In his words: "And I guess I want to go ahead and throw it in right now that the tetrasemaphores
shouldn't have restrictive language like that. It should just teach them why. You see?" Decided by Carey. It is why the project's own voice issues
no instructions, and it is the rule he applied again on 14 October to the flashcards.

### 2026-10-04 (Pacific) — Reusable layers, and the uncut ring
The release carries the layered assets so that a reader can rebuild the parts, and the uncut meander ring so that other languages can be added.
In his words: "part of this is to make it reusable. And by the way, because we're only going to do English and Spanish so far, that means that the
original uncut meanderings will have to still be a part of the package so that people could do other languages." Decided by Carey.

### 2026-10-05 — INFORMATIVO, not NO NORMATIVO
The Spanish word for the σᵥ state is INFORMATIVO. A literal translation of NON-NORMATIVE reads wrongly to a Spanish-speaking reader. Found in
translation review; decided by Carey. Listed in the failure-modes register as "a correct translation that is wrong for the reader".

### 2026-10-06 to 2026-10-07 — The "60 %" brain figure, dropped
Carey raised the familiar figure that people use only part of their brains. Google AI Mode built on it rather than correcting it. Claude Science
corrected it against the evidence, and Carey dropped it. **The AI corrected Carey**, and an AI failure is recorded beside it: Google AI Mode
agreed with its user instead of checking. Both rows are in the published failure-modes register.

### 2026-10-08 (Pacific) — Claim nothing the project is not
In his words: "Because I don't want to try to ever be anything we're not." Decided by Carey. It is the rule behind the published register of
failures and behind the test register's habit of labelling what has not been run.
